# High-Protocol Evening (Expanded)

A high-protocol evening uses consistent rules of speech, position, and service to create a clear container for several hours. This version breaks the evening into timed sections with specific dialogue options and the physical cues that invoke them.

## Minute-Level Structure Example (2.5–4 hours)

- 0:00–0:10 — Arrival, collar on, protocol activated with a spoken cue.
- 0:10–0:30 — Pre-dinner tasks (drinks, table setting, boot or gear attention).
- 0:30–1:15 — Dinner. Service of plates, limited speech, periodic position checks.
- 1:15–1:20 — Mid-point check-in (explicit pause).
- 1:20–1:50 — Continued service or quiet attendance.
- 1:50–2:05 — Formal close and protocol release.

## Dialogue Options & Invocation Methods

### Activating Protocol
**Options:**
- "Protocol begins now."
- "Collar on. You will use [title] until I release you."
- "Kneel. We start."

**How to invoke:**
- Fasten the collar, then speak the activation line.
- Point to the kneeling cushion as the first required action.
- Turn off overhead lights and leave only low lighting as the environmental cue.

### During Dinner Service
**Options:**
- "Pour."
- "You may speak."
- "Eyes down until I address you."

**How to invoke:**
- A small hand gesture toward the glass or plate.
- Touch their shoulder once to grant a short speaking window.
- Remain silent and wait; they serve only when you set down your fork or give a nod.

### Position Corrections
**Options:**
- "Adjust."
- "Closer."
- "Still."

**How to invoke:**
- A light tap on the cushion or floor.
- Point without speaking.
- Say the single word and then look away so they correct themselves.

### Ending Protocol
**Options:**
- "Protocol ends. You may speak freely."
- "Collar off. We're done."
- "Rise. Casual now."

**How to invoke:**
- Remove the collar yourself as the physical end signal.
- Stand, offer a hand, and speak the release line.
- Change the lighting or music back to normal as reinforcement.

## Why This Works
Long containers need frequent, low-effort cues. Single-word commands and consistent gestures let the evening stay formal without constant negotiation. Built-in check-ins prevent the protocol from becoming unsustainable.

## Modular Rules You Can Add
- Speech only when spoken to.
- Specific kneeling style at the table.
- Service tasks between courses.
- One allowed "may I" request per hour.
