# Full Boot Blacking Service Sequence

Boot blacking is one of the clearest service rituals in leather culture. Done well, it is meditative for both parties: the sub focuses on the work, the Dom receives care for their gear and the focused attention of the person at their feet. This sequence turns the task into a structured scene with clear transitions.

## Dialogue Options & Invocation Methods

### Calling to Service
**Options:**
- "Boots. Now."
- "Come here and take care of these."
- "Kneel. The supplies are ready."

**How to invoke:**
- Point to the mat beside the chair while seated, then wait.
- Place the boots on the floor in front of the kneeling position and say nothing until the sub is in place.
- Snap once and gesture downward.

### During Cleaning
**Options:**
- "Slower. Make sure you get the seams."
- "Good. Keep that pressure."
- "Show me the brush."

**How to invoke:**
- Rest a hand on the sub's shoulder or head while speaking quietly.
- Tap the boot lightly with a finger to indicate a spot that needs more attention.
- Remain silent for several minutes, then give one specific correction.

### Praise or Correction
**Options:**
- "Those are coming along well."
- "Start that section over."
- "Look up. Tell me what you're working on."

**How to invoke:**
- Use the sub's name or title only when delivering praise or a correction.
- Place the finished boot next to the unfinished one as a visual comparison.
- Have the sub present the brush or cloth for inspection before continuing.

### Final Presentation
**Options:**
- "Present them."
- "Stand and show me the result."
- "They're acceptable. You may rise."

**How to invoke:**
- Extend a hand or point upward as the signal to rise.
- Have the sub place both boots side-by-side on a cloth, then step back.
- Inspect in silence, then give a single nod or verbal acceptance.

## Why This Works
The physical focus of cleaning and polishing creates a natural drop into service headspace. Clear start and end cues prevent the task from feeling endless or vague. Specific feedback (rather than constant praise or silence) keeps the sub engaged and learning.

## Modular Building Blocks
- Silent vs. limited speech versions.
- Handler seated the entire time vs. walking around to inspect from different angles.
- Inclusion of a short "report" at the end where the sub describes what they did.
