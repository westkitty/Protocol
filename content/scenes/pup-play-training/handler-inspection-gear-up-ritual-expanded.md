# Handler Inspection & Gear-Up Ritual (Expanded)

This ritual turns putting on gear from a rushed logistic step into the psychological doorway of the scene. The inspection is not a checklist you rush through — it is the moment the pup stops being a person in clothes and becomes a pup under your hand. Every pause, every named piece of gear, every command reinforces the container.

## Dialogue Options & Invocation Methods

### 1. Beginning the Inspection
**Options:**
- "On your mat. Knees apart, hands behind your back, eyes down until I tell you to look up."
- "Present." (single word, followed by gesture)
- "Show me your posture."

**How to invoke:**
- Stand at the edge of the mat and point downward with two fingers, then wait in silence for 10–15 seconds.
- Snap fingers once and point to the mat.
- Say the full line while the pup is still standing, then step back so they have space to kneel.

### 2. Examining Gear
**Options:**
- "Leather's clean, buckles are straight. You took care of your gear — good pup."
- "This harness sits better when the straps are even. Fix it."
- "Show me the inside of the collar."

**How to invoke:**
- Pick up the piece of gear, hold it at eye level for the pup, then slowly turn it while speaking.
- Run your hand along the edge of the leather while describing what you find.
- Place the gear in the pup's mouth or paws briefly, then take it back to inspect.

### 3. Fitting the Collar
**Options:**
- "Chin up. Hold still."
- "Two fingers. That's the gap. Nod if it feels right."
- "This stays on until I take it off."

**How to invoke:**
- Stand behind the pup, place one hand on the back of the neck, and speak quietly into the ear.
- Hold the open collar in front of the pup's face so they can see it before you bring it around.
- Fasten it, then gently tug the D-ring once while making eye contact (if allowed).

### 4. Command Sequence
**Options:**
- "Sit. Stay. Don't move until I say."
- "Present. Good. Now down."
- "Heel." (followed by walking a short circle)

**How to invoke:**
- Use a closed fist held at chest height as the visual cue for "sit."
- Point to the floor for "down."
- Walk three steps away, then turn and wait — the pup must stay without further reminder.

### 5. Attaching the Leash
**Options:**
- "This clip means you're on my lead for the next hour. Nod if you're ready."
- "Leash on."
- "You're mine for this scene. Understand?"

**How to invoke:**
- Clip the leash while the pup is in "present," then give a single gentle tug.
- Hold the leash handle out and wait for the pup to nose it before clipping.
- Attach it, then walk a slow circle so the pup feels the weight of the lead.

### 6. Transition Cue
**Options:**
- "Inspection complete. Scene begins now."
- "Good pup. Let's begin."
- A single firm pat on the shoulder or hip.

**How to invoke:**
- Step back two paces, make eye contact, and speak the line clearly.
- Unclip and re-clip the leash as the physical signal that the ritual is over and play starts.
- Dim the lights further or change the music as the nonverbal transition.

## Why This Works
The slow, named handling of gear creates anticipation and clarity. The pup knows exactly when they are "on" because the transition is marked by physical action (collar, leash, command) rather than a vague feeling. Handlers who rush this step often find the pup never fully drops. The inspection is the scene.

## Common Pitfalls & Recovery
- Rushing the fitting: Pause, unbuckle, and re-do the collar with full attention.
- Pup fidgets during stillness: Correct once with a calm "Still," then reset the timer rather than escalating.
- Gear pinches: Stop immediately, adjust, and re-confirm the non-verbal signal before continuing.

## Modular Building Blocks
- Stillness holds: 30 seconds, 60 seconds, or 2 minutes between pieces.
- Mirror work: Pup watches themselves being fitted.
- Praise density: One specific compliment per gear piece vs. silence until the end.
- Correction style: Verbal only, light physical (tap on shoulder), or reset to presentation.
