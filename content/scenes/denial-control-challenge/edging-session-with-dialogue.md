# Structured Edging Session with Dialogue Control

Edging becomes more precise and intense when the verbal layer is intentional. This scene treats dialogue as a control tool: the bottom reports, requests, or stays silent according to agreed rules, and the top uses specific phrases to escalate, hold, or release.

## Dialogue Options & Invocation Methods

### Reporting Closeness
**Options the bottom may use:**
- "Close."
- "Yellow."
- "Please."

**How the top invokes the report:**
- "Tell me where you are." (spoken while slowing or pausing stimulation)
- A light tap on the thigh as the cue to speak.
- Require the report only when stimulation stops.

### Holding the Edge
**Options:**
- "Stay there."
- "Don't move."
- "Breathe."

**How to invoke:**
- Stop all movement and place a still hand on the body.
- Say the line once, then remain silent while holding position.
- Use a hand signal (open palm = hold) so speech is optional.

### Permission or Denial
**Options:**
- "Not yet."
- "You may."
- "Ruin it."

**How to invoke:**
- Speak the permission line only after the bottom has held still for a set count.
- Pair "Not yet" with resumed slow stimulation.
- For ruin: give the instruction, then remove hands and watch.

## Why This Works
Clear, limited vocabulary reduces ambiguity at the moment of highest arousal. The bottom knows exactly which words are useful; the top knows exactly when speech is required. This prevents both under-communication and constant begging that can break focus.

## Modular Building Blocks
- Silent edges (only non-verbal signals).
- Required begging before any permission.
- Timed holds (30 seconds, 60 seconds) announced in advance.
